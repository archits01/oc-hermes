INDEPENDENT AUDITORS’ REPORT

To the Members of Bangalore Electricity Supply Company Limited

Report on the Audit of the Financial Statements

Adverse Opinion

We have audited the accompanying financial statements of Bangalore Electricity Supply Company
Limited (‘the Company’), which comprise the Balance Sheet as at 31 March 2025, the Statement of
Profit and Loss (including Other Comprehensive Income), the Statement of Cash Flows and the
Statement of Changes in Equity for the year then ended and notes to the financial statements, including
a summary of the Material Accounting Policies and other explanatory information for the year ended
on that date (hereinafter referred to as ‘financial statements’).

In our opinion and to the best of our information and according to the explanations given to us, because
of the significance of the matters described in the Basis for Adverse Opinion section of our report, the
aforesaid financial statements do not give the information required by the Companies Act, 2013 (‘the
Act’) in the manner so required and do not give a true and fair view in conformity with the Indian
Accounting Standards prescribed under Section 133 of the Act read with the Companies (Indian
Accounting Standards) Rules, 2015, as amended, (‘Ind AS’) and other accounting principles generally
accepted in India, of the state of affairs of the Company as at 31 March 2025 and its loss (including
other comprehensive income), its cash flows and changes in equity for the year ended on that date.

Basis for Adverse Opinion

i.       Attention is drawn to material accounting policy no. 1 of the Company, wherein certain assets
         released from active use for repairs and maintenance are valued at Written Down Value
         (WDV). The released assets are brought back to the main block of assets when re-deployed
         again for active use, and depreciated on the straight-line method as applicable to other normal
         assets as if there was a fresh installation and revised useful life is not assessed at that stage.
         Consequently, the released assets are depreciated over a longer period as compared to the
         main block of assets. Also, the Company does not estimate the cost of dismantling and
         removing the item and restoring the site for arriving at the cost of capitalisation as required.
         Some of the assets capitalised during the year include assets that have been completed and
         commissioned in prior years and prior year depreciation of such assets are charged during
         the current year. The same is not in accordance with Ind AS 16 ‘Property, Plant and
         Equipment’. Consequent impact is not readily quantifiable in the absence of proper details.
         Our opinion on the financial statements for the year ended 31 March 2024 was also modified
         in respect of this matter.

         Further, as per Ind AS 16 and material accounting policy no. 1, costs directly attributable to
         bringing an asset to the location and condition necessary for it to be capable of operating in
         the manner intended by management shall be recognised as part of the cost of such asset.
         However, the Company is not completely recognising such directly attributable cost in the
         capital work-in-progress (CWIP) balances and subsequently in the cost of property, plant and
         equipment. Consequently, this has resulted in understatement of CWIP / property, plant and
         equipment in the balance sheet and depreciation expense in profit or loss and overstatement
         of expenses otherwise directly recognised in profit or loss.

       Further, as observed in some of the divisions, appropriate identification of different classes
       of property, plant and equipment and allocation of total contract consideration (purchase
       price) to such classes of property, plant and equipment are not accurate. Consequently, part
       of contract consideration has been incorrectly allocated to the asset category ‘miscellaneous

ii.    Attention is drawn to material accounting policy no. 8 of the Company wherein inventories
       are valued at Scheduled Standard Rates and the difference between purchase cost and
       Scheduled Standard Rate is recognised in Statement of Profit & Loss through Material Cost
       Variance account. However, the Company is not ascertaining net realisable value for items of
       inventory for the purpose of valuation. The same is not in accordance with Ind AS 2
       ‘Inventories’ which requires the inventories to be valued at lower of cost and net realisable
       value. Consequent impact of the same on accounts is not ascertainable. Our opinion on the
       financial statements for the year ended 31 March 2024 was also modified in respect of this
       matter.

iii.   Attention is drawn to material accounting policy no. 15.4 wherein provision for earned leave
       encashment including Half-Pay Leave (HPL) is made on the basis of estimates and not on the
       basis of actuarial valuation. The same is not in accordance with Ind AS 19 ‘Employee Benefits’
       and effect of the same on the accounts is not ascertainable. Our opinion on the financial
       statements for the year ended 31 March 2024 was also modified in respect of this matter.

iv.    Attention is drawn to material accounting policy no. 15.5 wherein the Company has not
       carried out actuarial valuation and accounted for expense / liability under ‘Family Benefit
       Fund’. The same is not in accordance with Ind AS 19 and effect of the same on the accounts
       is not ascertainable. Our opinion on the financial statements for the year ended 31 March
       2024 was also modified in respect of this matter.

v.     The Company contributes to KPTCL & ESCOMs Pension and Gratuity Trust (‘KEPGT’) towards
       gratuity & pension liability of its employees who have joined service on or before 31 March
       2006 and to KPTCL & ESCOMs Newly Defined Contributory Pension Scheme Family Pension
       & Gratuity Trust (‘KENFPGT’) towards gratuity and family pension in respect of employees
       who have joined the service on or after 1 April 2006, which are defined benefit schemes.
       Actuarial valuations are got done by the trusts at the end of each financial year. KEPGT /
       KENFPGT arrives at a percentage for contribution for the subsequent financial year based on
       actuarial valuation as at the end of March for the previous financial year, which is contributed
       to the trust by the Company. However, the contributions were based on actuarial valuation as
       on 31 March 2024 and liability based on actuarial valuation as on 31 March 2025 has not been
       ascertained and provided for in the financial statements for the year ended 31 March 2025
       (contributions were based on actuarial valuation as on 31 March 2023 and liability based on
       actuarial valuation as on 31 March 2024 has not been ascertained and provided for in the
       financial statements for the year ended 31 March 2024). To that extent the expenses and
       liability towards employee benefits are understated. Also, the disclosures required under Ind
       AS 19 ‘Employee Benefits’ have not been made. The same is not in accordance with Ind AS
       19 ‘Employee Benefits’ and consequent impact on the accounts is not ascertainable. Our
       opinion on the financial statements for the year ended 31 March 2024 was also modified in
       respect of this matter.

Meaning of Internal Financial Controls with reference to Financial Statements

A company's internal financial control with reference to financial statements is a process designed to
provide reasonable assurance regarding the reliability of financial reporting and the preparation of financial
statements for external purposes in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles. A
company's internal financial controls with reference to financial statements includes those policies and
procedures that (1) pertain to the maintenance of records that, in reasonable detail, accurately and fairly
reflect the transactions and dispositions of the assets of the company; (2) provide reasonable assurance
that transactions are recorded as necessary to permit preparation of financial statements in accordance
with generally accepted accounting principles, and that receipts and expenditures of the company are being
made only in accordance with authorisations’ of management and directors of the company; and (3)
provide reasonable assurance regarding prevention or timely detection of unauthorized acquisition, use, or
disposition of the company's assets that could have a material effect on the financial statements.

Inherent Limitations of Internal Financial Controls with reference to Financial Statements

Because of the inherent limitations of internal financial controls with reference to financial statements,
including the possibility of collusion or improper management override of controls, material misstatements
due to error or fraud may occur and not be detected. Also, projections of any evaluation of the internal
financial controls with reference to financial statements to future periods are subject to the risk that the
internal financial controls with reference to financial statements may become inadequate because of
changes in conditions, or that the degree of compliance with the policies or procedures may deteriorate.

xix.    The Company follows a system of recording journal entries (JEs) by clubbing multiple transactions
        while accounting in the software instead of recording separate JEs for each and every transaction.
        This potentially leads to difficulty in identifying and verifying any particular transaction. Also, the
        Company follows a system of accounting multiple accounts / borrowings pertaining to a single
        Bank / Lender under one GL head resulting in individual account / loan balances not getting
        identified separately in the books of account or trial balance. This may potentially lead to mistakes
        or omissions in a particular loan / bank account that may not get identified.

xx.     All payments of Rs. 5 lakhs and above are made by Cash Management Section (CMS) directly to
        contractors / parties, but CMS and accounting units account for the same as fund transferred to
        accounting units and funds received from CMS respectively. Further, accounting units pass bank
        entries for payment to contractor / creditor though payments are done from the Bank account of
        CMS. These entries are also reflected in cash books and Bank Reconciliation Statements (BRS) of
        the accounting units. This system of accounting creates notional bank entries in at accounting
        units-level and not an accepted practice of accounting.

xxi.    As per the Company’s internal policy, all payments above Rs. 5 lakhs must be made at the CMS to
        ensure appropriate oversight over those payments. However, few vendors / suppliers have been
        submitting multiple invoices just below Rs. 5 lakhs in respect of a distinctly identifiable service or
        transaction, which in aggregate, exceed the policy threshold. These multiple invoices are
        processed at the accounting unit-level even though, when aggregated, are to be processed at the
        CMS. This practice effectively circumvents the internal control policy for payments above Rs. 5
        lakhs.

xxii.   It is observed that the accounting units have not disclosed bank accounts used for pension &
        NDCPS payments in the books and accounted neither the pension amounts released from the
        KEPGT / KENFPGT to the units nor payment of pension amounts to the retired employees / family
        pensioners even though such bank accounts are operated by the respective accounting units. The
        initial deposits made with the Banks at the time of account opening are reflected under ‘Deposits
        with Others’ under ‘Other Non-Current Assets’ instead of ‘Cash & Cash Equivalents’. Also, the Bank
        accounts used for statutory tax payments are not disclosed in the financial statements. The
        procedure followed by the Company potentially misstates the bank balances, liability for unpaid
        pension and statutory dues at year end.

xxiii.    Some of the material accounting policies selected by the Company are not applied — non-
          ascertainment of fair values for financial instruments, non-recognition of right-of-use assets
          (leases), non-recognition of regulatory deferral account balances, not retrospectively restating the
          financial statements, are not in compliance with applicable Ind AS and may potentially result in
          misstatement in the financial statements. Also, the Company does not have an accounting policy
          for recognising power banking / swapping arrangements carried with power supply entities in
          other states.

xxiv.     The system of accounting for imprest balances is inappropriate as amounts utilised out of imprest
          balances are being recognised in disbursement bank account ledgers rather than adjusting
          through imprest ledgers. Also, recoupment of imprest balances is not being adjusted through

A ‘material weakness’ is a deficiency, or a combination of deficiencies, in internal financial controls with
reference to financial statements, such that there is a reasonable possibility that a material misstatement
of the Company's annual or interim financial statements will not be prevented or detected on a timely basis.

the achievement of the objectives of the control criteria, the Company has not maintained in all material
